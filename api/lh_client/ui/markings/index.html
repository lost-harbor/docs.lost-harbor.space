Module markings 

Expand description

Paint, registry and repair marks for the drawn hull views. Paint and wear on a hull: the livery, the working marks and the repair history that the drawn views lay over the plate.

The ship research says markings answer three reading distances. From far away a viewer sees a colour mass and one recognition sign. Closer in, the registry block and the hazard bands show. At the hull, the stamps of old repairs show. Every mark here is tied to a part of the ship: a module end, the inhabited core, the drive or a structural joint. Nothing is scattered at random over the plate.

Wear follows the same rule. A few events explain the age of a hull: a plate patched at a joint, a module swapped from another hull, a new name painted over the old one, soot behind the drive. Each event leaves its own marks, so an old hull tells a story and does not just look dirty.

The marks live in ship space, along and across the ship, with a height fraction for the side. The top view and the side view each project the same list, so a bar that crosses the roof also crosses the flank at the same station. This is the Strategy pattern in data form: one list of marks, and one projection per view.
